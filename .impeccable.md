# Akzent design context

An English-speaking learner compares Gothic words with Sanskrit accent evidence,
proposes a rule, records a prediction, and keeps the comparisons that resist it.
The second task reworks Verner's explicitly fictional *akasatam. It makes three
consonants change in response to one moving accent. Nothing implies Sanskrit is
the ancestor of Gothic, or that a reconstructed form was found in a manuscript.

The visual center is a pair of oversized word forms, aligned over their Sanskrit
comparanda. A dental is set inside a small contrast field so it remains readable
without color. The experiment uses movable syllables and a visible sequence.
No ancient scenery, decorative maps, stock photographs, dashboard cards, grades,
or fictitious handwriting.

Palette: chalk #faf9ff, lilac #ebe5f6, ink #302342, plum #634087,
teal #17665c, amber #83510c. Dark: #211a2c, #30273f, #f1edf8.
Gentium Plus gives real diacritic coverage to word forms and source excerpts;
Hanken Grotesk carries controls and explanation. Left-aligned English, word pairs
on a shared baseline. Small asymmetries come from word length and annotations.

On phones the words stay side by side where legible, then controls and evidence
stack. Native selects, radios, checkboxes, buttons, and a keyboard-pannable source
image remain usable. Content width and type must not shrink to fit a desktop
diagram. Dark mode follows the system or an explicit choice; print stays light.

The first design idea was a philological broadsheet with hairline rules and a
cream field. That would repeat much of the collection. This design uses a lilac
comparison surface and the actual accent as the main visual operation.
